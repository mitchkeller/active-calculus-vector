Exercise 6.

Verify that the parameterization \(\vr_1(s)\) of the curve \(C\) in Example 10.3.10 has unit speed.
🔗
Solution.
If we are diligent and persistent with our calculations, we can show that \(\vecmag{\vr_{1}'}=1\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} \vr_{1}' =\amp \left\langle 2(-2+\sqrt{4+s})\frac{1/2}{\sqrt{4+s}} \right., \\ \amp \quad \quad \left. \frac{8}{3}\left(\frac{3}{2}\left(-2+\sqrt{4+s}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1/2}{\sqrt{4+s}}\right)\right) , 4 \frac{1/2}{\sqrt{4+s}} \right\rangle \\ =\amp \frac{1}{\sqrt{4+s}} \left\langle -2+\sqrt{4+s},2\sqrt{-2+\sqrt{4+s}},2 \right\rangle \\ \vecmag{\vr_{1}'} =\amp \frac{1}{\sqrt{4+s}} \sqrt{\left( \left(-2+\sqrt{4+s}\right)^2+\left(2\sqrt{-2+\sqrt{4+s}}\right)^2+(2)^2 \right)} \\ =\amp \frac{1}{\sqrt{4+s}} \sqrt{\left( (-2+\sqrt{4+s})+2\right)^2} \\ =\amp \sqrt{\frac{4+s}{4+s}} = 1 \end{align*}
