Activity 12.3.5.

Let \(D\) be the triangular region with vertices \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((4,0)\text{,}\) and \((0,2)\text{.}\) Consider the double integral \(\displaystyle{\iint_D (4-x-2y) \, dA}\text{.}\)
🔗

(a)

Draw and label a plot of \(D\) with relevant cross sections for a vertically simple description. Give the inequalities that show \(D\) is vertically simple.
🔗
Solution.
Integrating with respect to \(y\) first means we take cross sections of \(D\) parallel to the \(y\)-axis. As the figure below illustrates, the lower end of any cross section is always on the \(x\)-axis (with \(y=0\)) and the upper limit is on the line joining \((4,0)\) and \((0,2)\text{.}\) The limits on \(y\) need to written in terms of \(x\text{,}\) so the upper limit is \(y = 2-\frac{1}{2}x\text{.}\) These cross sections run from \(x=0\) to \(x=4\text{,}\) so as an iterated integral we have
\begin{equation*} \iint_D \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)(4-x-2y) \, dy \, dx = \int_{0}^{4} \int_{0}^{2-(1/2)x} \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)(4-x-2y) \, dy \, dx \end{equation*}

(b)

Write the double integral as an iterated integral of the form \(\displaystyle \iint_D (4-x-2y) \, dy \, dx\text{,}\) including correct bounds on the integral.
🔗
Solution.
\begin{equation*} \iint_D \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)(4-x-2y) \, dy \, dx = \int_{0}^{4} \int_{0}^{2-(1/2)x} \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)(4-x-2y) \, dy \, dx \end{equation*}

(c)

Evaluate the iterated integrals from the previous part. Write a sentence describing at least one interpretation of the meaning of the value of the double integral.
🔗
Solution.
Evaluating the iterated integral with respect to \(y\) then \(x\) gives us
\begin{align*} \iint_D \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)(4-x-2y) \, dy \, dx \amp = \int_{0}^{4} \int_{0}^{2-(1/2)x} \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)(4-x-2y) \, dy \, dx\\ \amp = \frac{3}{4} \int_{0}^{4} \left[(4-x)y-y^2\right] \mid_{0}^{2-(1/2)x} \, dx\\ \amp = \frac{3}{4} \int_{0}^{4} \left[(4-x)\left(2-\frac{1}{2}x\right)-\left(2-\frac{1}{2}x\right)^2\right] \, dx\\ \amp = \frac{3}{4} \int_{0}^{4} \left[4-2x+\frac{1}{4}x^2\right] \, dx\\ \amp = \frac{3}{4} \left[4x-x^2+\frac{1}{12}x^3\right] \mid_{0}^{4}\\ \amp = \frac{3}{4} \left(\frac{16}{3}\right)\\ \amp = 4 \end{align*}
Since \(f\) is non-negative on the domain \(D\text{,}\) this iterated integral tell us the volume of the solid bounded above by the graph of \(f(x,y) = \frac{3}{4}(4-x-2y)\) and below by the triangular region \(D\text{.}\)
