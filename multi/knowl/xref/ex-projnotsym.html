Exercise 12.

In this exercise, we will show that the roles of \(\vu\) and \(\vv\) are not symmetric in projection formula (Definition 9.3.10). We will use the same values as Example 9.3.12, namely \(\vu = \langle 4,3\rangle \) and \(\vv = \langle 0,2 \rangle\text{,}\) but we will be looking at the projection of \(\vv\) onto \(\vu\text{.}\)
🔗

(a)

Will \(proj_\vu \vv\) be parallel to \(\vu\) or \(\vv\text{?}\) Use this answer to explain how you know that the result of \(proj_\vu \vv\) must be different than \(proj_\vv \vu\) (which we calculated in Example 9.3.12).
🔗
Solution.
The vector \(proj_\vu \vv\) will be parallel to \(\vu\text{.}\) Similarly, \(proj_\vv \vu\) will be parallel to \(\vv\text{.}\) So \(proj_\vu \vv\) and \(proj_\vv \vu\) will be not be in the same direction and will be very different vectors.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Use Definition 9.3.10 to calculate \(comp_\vu \vv\text{,}\) \(proj_{\vu} \vv\text{,}\) and \(proj_{\perp \vu} \vv\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
The projection of \(\vv\) in the direction of \(\vv\) will be given by
\begin{equation*} proj_{\vu} \vv = \frac{\vu \cdot \vv}{\vu \cdot \vu} \vu = \frac{6}{25} \langle 4,3\rangle\text{.} \end{equation*}
The parallel component of \(\vv\) in the direction of \(\vv\) will be given by
\begin{equation*} comp_{\vu} \vv = \frac{\vu \cdot \vv}{\vecmag{vu}} = \frac{6}{5}\text{.} \end{equation*}
The orthogonal projection of \(\vv\) in the direction of \(\vv\) will be given by
\begin{equation*} proj_{\perp \vu} \vv = \langle 0,2 \rangle - \frac{6}{25} \langle 4,3\rangle = \langle - \frac{6}{25} , \frac{32}{25} \rangle \text{.} \end{equation*}
