Preview Activity 12.3.1.

A tetrahedron is a three-dimensional figure with four faces, each of which is a triangle. A picture of the tetrahedron \(T\) with vertices \((0,0,0)\text{,}\) \((1,0,0)\text{,}\) \((0,1,0)\text{,}\) and \((0,0,1)\) is shown in Figure 12.3.1.
🔗

(a)

Notice that the top surface of the tetrahedron is part of the plane through three vertices of the tetrahedron. Find an equation of this plane that has \(z\) on one side of the equation by itself.
🔗
🔗

(b)

In single-variable calculus, you used integrals to find the volume of three-dimensional solids by slicing the solid into shapes (e.g., discs, washers, or cylindrical shells) whose volumes could be computed using formulas from geometry. We can do the same here by recognizing that a cross section of the tetrahedron parallel to the \(yz\)-plane, which is the intersection of the plane \(x=a\) with the tetrahedron, is a triangle. This cross-section is shown in Figure 12.3.2.
🔗
Create carefully-labeled two-dimensional drawings of the highlighted cross section in Figure 12.3.2 for \(a=1/3\) and \(a=1/2\text{.}\) Your drawings should be in the \(yz\)-plane.
🔗
🔗

(c)

The cross-sections in part b have area that could be found using the formula for the area of a triangle. However, we will ultimately consider cross-sections where this is not the case but where the area can be found using a single-variable integral. Write a \(dy\) integral for the area of each cross-section in part b.
🔗
🔗

(d)

Generalize your work in the previous two parts to create a carefully-labeled two-dimensional drawing of the highlighted cross section in Figure 12.3.2 that can be interpreted for any value of \(a\) where the plane \(x=a\) intersects the tetrahedron. Your drawing should be in the \(yz\)-plane, and you should label components of your diagram in terms of the parameter \(a\text{.}\) Also write a \(dy\) integral for the area of the cross-section. Your integral will include \(a\) in multiple ways.
🔗
🔗

(e)

The cross-sections that we have been considering can become three-dimensional solids by giving each a small amount of thickness in the \(x\)-direction. We can denote this thickness by \(dx\text{,}\) which means that multiplying the integral you obtained in the previous part by \(dx\) gives the volume of a slice of the tetrahedron. The base \(R\) of the tetrahedron is shown in Figure 12.3.3, with the location of the cross-section corrsponding to \(x=a\) indicated.
🔗
The volume of the tetrahedron can be computed using the double integral
\begin{equation*} \iint_R 1-(x+y) dA \end{equation*}
where the region of integration is the triangle at the base of the tetrahedron in the \(xy\)-plane as shown in Figure 12.3.3. We must express this double integral as an iterated integral using the cross-sectional ideas from Section 12.2.
🔗
In the previous part, you wrote a \(dy\) integral that expresses the area of the cross-section with \(x=a\text{.}\) After replacing \(a\) by \(x\text{,}\) this integral becomes the inner integral in the iterated integral that computes the volume of the tetrahedron. Use Figure 12.3.3 to determine the values of \(x\) for which you will need to evaluate the outer integral in an iterated integral to find the volume of the tetrahedron. Write a sentence to explain how you arrived at these bounds.
