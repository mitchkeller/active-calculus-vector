Activity 10.1.4.

For this activity we will consider the paraboloid with equation \(z = x^2+y^2\text{.}\) You do not need to know what the graph of \(z=x^2+y^2\) looks like at this point.
🔗

(a)

Parameterize the intersection of the paraboloid with the fundamental plane \(x=2\text{.}\) What type of curve is the intersection?
🔗
Solution.
\(f(2, y) = 4 + y^2 = z\text{.}\) Let \(y(t) = t\text{.}\) Then \(\vr(t) = \langle 2, t, 4 + t^2 \rangle\text{.}\) This is a parabola in the \(yz\)-plane at \(x = 2\) that opens up in the positive \(z\)-direction with a vertex of \((2, 0, 4)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Parameterize the intersection of the paraboloid with the fundamental plane \(y = -1\text{.}\) What type of curve is the intersection?
🔗
Solution.
\(f(x, -1) = x^2 + 1 = z\text{.}\) Let \(x(t) = t\text{.}\) Then \(\vr(t) = \langle t, -1, t^2 + 1 \rangle\text{.}\) This is a parabola in the \(xz\)-plane at \(y = -1\) that opens up in the positive \(z\)-direction with a vertex of \((0, -1, 1)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

Parameterize the intersection of the paraboloid with the fundamental plane \(z = 25\text{.}\) What type of curve is the intersection?
🔗
Solution.
\(25 = x^2 + y^2\text{.}\) Let \(x(t) = t\text{.}\) Then \(\vr(t) = \langle t, \sqrt{25 - t^2}, 25 \rangle\) and \(\vr(t) = \langle t, -\sqrt{25 - t^2}, 25 \rangle\text{.}\) Or a circle with radius of 5 is defined by \(\langle 5\cos(t), 5\sin(t) \langle.\text{.}\) Then \(\vr(t) = \langle 5\cos(t), 5\sin(t), 25 \rangle\text{.}\) This is a circle with radius \(5\) in the plane \(z = 25\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

How do your responses change to all three of the preceding questions if you instead consider the surface defined by \(z = x^2 - y^2\text{?}\)
🔗
Hint.
For generating one of the parameterizations you may want to consider \(\sec^2(t)-\tan^2(t) = 1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The parabola in (a) opens down. The parabola in (b) shifts down and the vertex is \((0, -1, -1)\text{.}\) The function in (c) is a hyperbola.
