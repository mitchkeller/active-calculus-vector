Preview Activity 12.2.1.

In this activity we will explore the double integral of \(f(x,y) = 25-x^2-y^2\) on the rectangular domain \(R\) containing the points that satisfy \(-3\leq x\leq 3\) and \(-4\leq y\leq 4\text{.}\) As with partial derivatives, we may treat one of the variables in \(f\) as constant and think of the resulting function as a function of a single variable. We will now investigate what this means geometrically and algebraically when we integrate with one input variable fixed.
🔗

(a)

Let \(a\) be a fixed value in the interval \([-3,3]\text{.}\) Compute
\begin{equation*} \int_{-4}^4 f(a,y) dy \text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
Solution.
We will integrate in terms of \(y\) to get
\begin{equation*} \int_{-4}{^4} 25-a^2-y^2 \, dy = 25 (8)-8a^2-y^3/3 \restrict{-4}^4 = 200-8a^2-128/3 \end{equation*}

(b)

The answer of the previous part depended on the value of \(a\text{,}\) but the process would be the exact same set of steps for every \(a\text{.}\) This allows us to define a function \(A(x)\) as
\begin{equation*} A(x) = \int_{-4}^4 f(x,y) \, dy\text{.} \end{equation*}
Write a couple of sentences to explain the geometric meaning of the value of \(A(x)\text{.}\) Your explanation should refer to different parts of Figure 12.2.2, including the surface defined by \(f\) and the trace determined by the fixed value of \(x\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Since \(f(x,y) \geq 0\) on the given domain, the value of \(A(x)\) tells us the area under the \(y\)-trace curve for that fixed value of \(x\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(c)

Write a couple of sentences explaining the geometric meaning of \(A(x_i^*) \ \Delta x\text{,}\) where \(x_i^*\) is a fixed value of \(x\text{.}\) Your explanation should refer to the parts of Figure 12.2.3.
🔗
Solution.
Since \(A(x_i^*)\) is an area under the \(y\)-trace for the value \(x = x_i^*\text{,}\) when we multiply that area by a constant width \(\Delta x\text{,}\) we obtain the volume of a slab obtained by thickening the region under the \(x=x_i^*\) trace by a uniform thickness \(\Delta x\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(d)

Since \(f\) is continuous on \(R\text{,}\) we can define the function \(A = A(x)\) at every value of \(x\) in \([-3,3]\text{.}\) Think about subdividing the \(x\)-interval \([-3,3]\) into \(m\) subintervals and choosing a value \(x_i^*\) in each of those subintervals. Write a sentence describing the meaning of the sum \(\sum_{i=1}^m A(x_i^*) \ \Delta x\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
This sum will give us the sum of volumes of slabs with constant cross sections parallel to the \(yz\)-plane, where the cross sections look like the areas under the graphs of the \(x_i^*\) traces.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(e)

Write a couple of sentences to explain why \(\displaystyle\int_{-3}^3 A(x) \, dx\) determines the exact value of the volume under the surface \(z = f(x,y)\) over the rectangle \(R\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
As we let \(m\) go to infinity, the thickness of the slabs goes to 0 and we are just adding up all of the cross sectional areas of the surface parallel to the \(yz\)-plane, and so
\begin{equation*} \int_{-3}^3 A(x) \, dx = \lim_{m \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^m A(x_i^*) \ \Delta x \end{equation*}
gives us the volume of the solid under the surface \(z = f(x,y)\) over the rectangle \(R\text{.}\)
