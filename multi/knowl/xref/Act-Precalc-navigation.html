(c)

In the context of this problem, explain why you can add the horizontal components of \(\overrightarrow{CA}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AS}\) to get the horizontal component of \(\overrightarrow{CS}\text{.}\) Write a sentence or two about why this argument should work for the vertical components as well.
🔗
Solution.
When we look at the horizontal components of vectors in this problem, we are looking how we are traveling in the Eas/West direction only. So the amount we go in the East/West direction from \(C\) to \(A\) can be added to the amount we go in the East/West direction from \(A\) to \(S\) to find the amount we go in the East/West direction from \(C\) to \(S\text{.}\) We can separate our travel in two dimensions into a displacement argument in each direction (East/West or North/South).

(d)

After hiking to the location suggested by the ranger, you still don’t see your friend and call the ranger station again. The regional manager of rangers answers this time. She says the first ranger made a mistake in their navigation. They sent a drone to the location of your car and the drone spotted your friend along the same direction from your car to point \(A\text{,}\) but your friend is three times as far away from your car as you were when you were at point \(A\text{.}\)
🔗
In order to avoid confusion or any other mistakes, you want to compute the vector from your car to the drone’s suggested location, which we will call \(D\text{.}\) What are the components of \(\overrightarrow{CD}\text{?}\) Write a sentence or two to compare this vector to \(\overrightarrow{CA}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Because \(D\) is in the same direction from \(C\) toward \(A\text{,}\) but three times farther, we can go in the direction given by \(\overrightarrow{CA}\) three times. In other words, we can go three km east and four km north, then go three km east and 4 km north, then go three km east and 4 km north again. In other words, we want to do three times \(\overrightarrow{CA}\) to get \(\overrightarrow{CD}=\langle 9,12 \rangle \text{.}\)
🔗

(e)

So we need to get from point \(S\) to point \(D\text{.}\) Explain how to use the components of \(\overrightarrow{CS}\) and \(\overrightarrow{CD}\) to find the components of \(\overrightarrow{SD}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Because \(\overrightarrow{CS}\) and \(\overrightarrow{CD}\) describe the displacement from the same location to \(S\) and \(D\text{,}\) we can look at the componentwise difference between these vectors to get the displacement from \(S\) to \(D\text{.}\) The displacement in the East/West direction from \(S\) to \(D\) will be the difference in the first components of the vectors \(\overrightarrow{CS}\) and \(\overrightarrow{CD}\) and similarly for the second components to be used to find the North/South displacement. So, to find the components of
\begin{equation*} \overrightarrow{SD}= \overrightarrow{CD}-\overrightarrow{CS}=\langle 9,12 \rangle -\langle 1,5 \rangle = \langle 8,7 \rangle \end{equation*}

(f)

You find your friend at point \(D\) and want to take them back to your car to go home. Explain how you can use the components of \(\overrightarrow{CD}\) to give directions from point \(D\) back to your car.
🔗
Solution.
Since \(\overrightarrow{CD}=langle 9,12\rangle\text{,}\) this means we can travel the same components in reverse to get from point \(D\) back to our car. Algebraically, this means that \(-\overrightarrow{CD}=langle -9,-12\rangle\text{,}\) or we travel 9 km west and 12 km south to get back to our car.
