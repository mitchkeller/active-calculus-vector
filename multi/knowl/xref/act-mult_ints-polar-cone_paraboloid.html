Activity 12.5.3.

Use polar coordinates to find the volume of the solid between the cone \(z=f(x,y)=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) and the paraboloid \(z=g(x,y)=\frac{1}{8}\left(x^2+y^2\right) + 2\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
In polar coordinates, we have \(f(r,\theta) = r\) and \(g(r,\theta) = \frac{r^2}{8} + 2\text{.}\) To see where the cone and the paraboloid intersect, we set the equations equal to one another:
\begin{align*} r \amp = \frac{r^2}{8} + 2\\ 8r \amp = r^2 + 16 \\ 0 \amp = r^2 -8r + 16\\ r \amp = \frac{8\pm \sqrt{8^2-4\cdot 16}}{2} = \frac{8}{2} = 4 \end{align*}
Thus, the solid lies entirely above the disc \(D\) with \(0\leq r\leq 4\) (and \(0\leq \theta\leq 2\pi\)). We have that \(g\) is the upper surface of the solid and \(f\) is the lower surface. The volume is thus found by
\begin{align*} \iint_D (g-f)\, dA \amp = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^4 \left(\frac{r^2}{8}+2 + r\right) r\, dr\, d\theta\\ \amp = \int_0^{2\pi} \left(\frac{r^4}{32} + r^2 + \frac{r^3}{3}\right)\restrict{0}^4\, d\theta\\ \amp = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{138}{3}\, d\theta = \frac{276}{3}\pi\text{.} \end{align*}
